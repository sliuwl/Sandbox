Two-body motion: relative ellipse vs. actual particle orbits

The relative coordinate r = r1 - r2 follows a Kepler ellipse. But in the center-of-mass frame, both particles move: neither particle is fixed unless one mass is taken to infinity.

What the animation shows

The green point is the center of mass, fixed here because we are viewing the center-of-mass inertial frame. The blue and orange particles orbit this point on scaled ellipses.

The purple dashed ellipse is the orbit of the relative coordinate r. It is not the same as the orbit of either particle about the center of mass.

Relations being visualized

With M = m1 + m2 and center of mass at the origin,

r1 = (m2/M) r,   r2 = -(m1/M) r.

The minus sign is important: particle 2 is always on the opposite side of the center of mass from particle 1.
